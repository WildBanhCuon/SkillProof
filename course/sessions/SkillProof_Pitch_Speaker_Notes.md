## Slide 1 — Title & hook (~30 seconds)

**On screen:** SkillProof logo · *Too many applicants. Not enough proof.*

**Say:**

> Good morning. We’re **[team names]**, and we built **SkillProof**.
>
> Our hook is simple: **too many applicants, not enough proof.**
>
> SkillProof helps hiring teams turn a flood of junior applications into a **verified, job-ready shortlist** — with evidence they can defend, not guesswork from CVs.

*Pause half a second. Don’t rush into problem.*

---

## Slide 2 — Problem (~1 minute)

**On screen:** *One junior role. Hundreds of look-alike CVs.* · **28–35** hours

**Say:**

> Here’s the pain we kept hearing from HR leads.
>
> Marion runs talent at an **80-person scale-up**. She posts **one junior frontend role** — and within days she has **two or three hundred applications**. On paper, they all look the same: same bootcamps, same keywords, portfolios polished by ChatGPT.
>
> Her real question isn’t “who has a nice CV?” It’s **“which of these people can actually do the job?”**
>
> Today she spends roughly **28 to 35 hours** of recruiter time per hire just screening — before anyone qualified reaches a technical interview.
>
> Many teams try **AI on résumés**. It’s fast, but it still learns from **who you hired in the past** — school, pedigree, keywords — not from demonstrated skill on **this** role.
>
> So you get speed without **proof**, and decisions that are hard to explain when a hire fails or a candidate asks why they were rejected.

**One-liner (optional, strong delivery):**

> We don’t ask “does this CV look good?” We ask **“can this person do the job?”**

---

## Slide 3 — Solution (~1 minute)

**On screen:** **Upgrade ad → Prove skills → Rank evidence**

**Say:**

> SkillProof is deliberately **not** another ATS. We don’t replace Greenhouse or Workable.
>
> We own the **wedge** between “we posted a junior role” and “here’s who we should interview.”
>
> **Step one — upgrade the job ad.** AI flags vague or unrealistic requirements and builds a **skill matrix** for that specific role. Better ads attract better-fit applicants.
>
> **Step two — candidates prove skills.** Each role gets a **fresh assessment** generated from that job — coding and multiple choice — not a generic test bank from last year.
>
> Candidates can take a **practice test** with a **different question set**, so they learn the format without leaking answers into the real application.
>
> **Step three — HR gets ranked evidence.** Every applicant is scored on **rubrics** with cited strengths and risks — ready now, trainable, or at risk — not a black-box “match percentage.”
>
> The buyer is the **Head of Talent or HR lead** at companies around **50 to 500 employees**. Recruiters use it daily; hiring managers trust the shortlist because it’s **defensible**.

## Slide 4 — Live demo (~3 minutes)

**On screen:** Four lines only · then **switch to the app** (skillproof-z3j9.onrender.com)

**Say before you share screen:**

> I’ll show the happy path in about three minutes. We’ve pre-loaded data; if the network fails we have a backup video.

### Step 1 — Check listing (~45 s)

**Do:** HR → open a **published junior role** → click **Check listing**.

**Say:**

> First, HR runs **Check listing**. The model reads the ad and returns **concrete issues** — seniority mismatch, vague responsibilities — plus a **skills matrix** aligned to the role.
>
> *Bad ads attract the wrong crowd; we fix that first.*

### Step 2 — Practice test (~45 s)

**Do:** Log in or switch to **candidate** (e.g. sofiane@test.com) → open same job → start **practice** session → show one question briefly (don’t complete whole test unless time).

**Say:**

> On the candidate side, they complete a short profile, then can try a **practice test**. Important: **practice uses different questions** than the real application — so exploring the format doesn’t cheat the hiring test.

### Step 3 — Apply & submit (~60 s)

**Do:** Start **application** session → answer or skim → **Submit** → show grading / result page (dimension scores, feedback).

**Say:**

> For a real application, they take the **application test**, submit, and our backend runs **batch grading** — multiple choice scored deterministically, open answers graded with **Gemini** against the rubric.
>
> The candidate sees **structured feedback**, not silence.

### Step 4 — Ranked shortlist (~45 s) — **memorable moment**

**Do:** Back to **HR** → job **Results** → top candidate card → expand evidence / recommendation.

**Say:**

> This is the moment we want you to remember: the **HR dashboard** — candidates **ranked by evidence**, with dimension scores and a clear recommendation.
>
> Marion can walk into a meeting with her CTO and say: **“Here are the five people worth interviewing — and here’s why.”**
>
> That’s the shortlist that used to take two weeks of inbox triage.

**Return to slides** when demo ends.

## Slide 5 — Why AI (~1 minute)

**On screen:** `Job ad → Gemini → Assessment → Grade → Rank`

**Say:**

> You might ask: **why is AI necessary?**
>
> Without it, this product doesn’t work at scale.
>
> You cannot manually **rewrite and analyze** every job ad, **generate a new calibrated test** per posting, and **apply consistent rubric grading** across sixty or more submissions — not at a cost any SMB would pay.
>
> Our pipeline is: **job ad in**, **Gemini** for analysis and generation, **assessment**, **grade on submit**, **ranked output**.
>
> What stays **human**: HR **approves** listing changes, **publishes** the role, and makes the **final hire decision**. Under the EU AI Act, employment screening is **high-risk AI** — we design for **oversight and transparency**, not autonomous rejection.
>
> And if models get cheaper tomorrow, we still win on the **workflow and the evidence** — not on owning the API.

## Slide 6 — Market (~45 seconds)

**On screen:** TAM **$5.25B** · SAM **$1.84B** · SOM **$9.2M**

**Say:**

> On market size — these are **scenario-based** estimates, not claims we’ve already captured.
>
> Broad **TAM** for hiring-assessment and screening software is on the order of **five billion dollars** annually.
>
> Our **serviceable** wedge: companies with **50 to 500 employees** hiring **junior tech talent** — about **1.8 billion**.
>
> What we believe we can reach in **12 to 24 months** with focused outbound is on the order of **nine million** — still a meaningful SaaS business.
>
> Competitors fall into three buckets: **ATS tools with resume AI** — fast but CV-centric; **coding test platforms** — often annual contracts and painful per-candidate fees at volume; and the **manual default** — spreadsheets and gut feel.
>
> We’re not winning by being another chatbot. We win with **one workflow**, **job-linked tests**, and **explainable rubrics**.

## Slide 7 — Business model (~45 seconds)

**On screen:** **€299 / month** · Growth tier · *Paid pilots first*

**Say:**

> We charge a **monthly subscription** based on **active job slots** and **graded candidates per month** — with **unlimited recruiter seats** so adoption isn’t blocked by seat math.
>
> Our **Growth** tier for a typical **50-to-100 person** company is **299 euros per month** — five active jobs, three hundred graded candidates.
>
> Plans run from about **149 to 899 euros** depending on volume.
>
> We’re **not** pricing on API cost — grading is cents per candidate. We price on **recruiter time saved** — that **28-to-35-hour** screening tax we showed earlier.
>
> Go-to-market: we start with **paid pilots** — five hundred to fifteen hundred euros for a few weeks on a live role — prove hours saved and shortlist quality, then convert to annual-style monthly contracts. Founder-led outbound first, then case studies and referrals.

---

## Slide 8 — Risks (~1 minute)

**On screen:** Market L4 · Tech L3 · Adoption L4

**Say:**

> We’re explicit about risks — naming them is a strength.
>
> **Market — likelihood four:** teams say they love it but won’t pay. We mitigate with **paid pilots** and hard **ROI metrics** — hours saved, interview quality — before annual commits.
>
> **Technical — likelihood three:** AI quality, latency, or cost drift. We use **schema validation**, logging, retries, and room for **human review** when confidence is low.
>
> **Adoption — likelihood four:** HR already has an ATS and doesn’t want another tool. We position as a **wedge** — keep your ATS; add SkillProof only for **verification**. ATS export is on our roadmap.
>
> **Regulatory:** EU AI Act compliance for hiring AI is real; we’re building toward documentation, candidate disclosure, and human oversight — not claiming we’re audit-complete in this school MVP.
>
> The trade-off we own is **quality at speed**: automate the repetitive first pass; go deeper when the model is uncertain.

## Quick Q&A bullets (if asked — not on slides)

| Question | Short answer |
|----------|----------------|
| **Market too big / too small?** | Scenario ranges; we focus the wedge — junior evidence, 50–500 employees — not all HR software. |
| **Free GPT tomorrow?** | Edge is workflow + rubric evidence + practice/application integrity — not API access. |
| **Why pay vs Codility / TestGorilla?** | Job-linked generation, predictable monthly volume, explainable scores — between cheap tests and full ATS upgrade. |
| **Sandbox / run code?** | MVP grades submitted code with AI + rubrics; live execution sandbox is a deliberate cut we’d add if buyers require it. |
| **GDPR / AI Act?** | High-risk hiring AI — disclosure, human final decision, logging; full compliance is post-pilot work. |
| **Revenue today?** | School prototype; demo billing only; pilots are the first real revenue test. |
